Modes

Three ways to stay in charge

Start relaxed and switch off only what you distrust, or start strict and let in only what you approve. Change modes any time from the menu bar.

Blocklist

Everything works as usual. You switch off only the apps and addresses you don't trust.

App allowlist

Every app asks first. Approve it once, for an hour, or forever — or say no.

Domain & IP allowlist

Go further: every unfamiliar domain or IP needs your OK. Or trust an app completely with one click.

Menu bar

Always one click away.

The menu bar panel lists the apps you have open, with live download and upload speeds and a switch for each. Block one in a second.

  • Live speeds for your whole Mac and for each app
  • Blocked apps are pinned to the top in red, so a switch flipped by accident can't hide
  • Change the mode without opening the window
Privacy report & log

See who's talking to whom.

The Privacy Report ranks your apps by how many domains they contact, and domains by how many apps reach them. Tap an app to see every address it talked to over the last 7 days.

  • Activity shows connections that are open right now, plus the last 5 minutes
  • Keep a full connection log for 7, 14 or 30 days — or cap it at 5, 10 or 200 MB
  • The size cap is a real ceiling: the oldest entries are trimmed so the log never grows past it
  • Search it, export it as CSV, clear it whenever you like

Getting started

Up and running in a minute

Turn on protection

Open Netoff and switch protection on. It installs a small network extension.

Allow the extension

macOS asks for your approval once: System Settings › General › Login Items & Extensions › Network Extensions.

Pick a mode

Choose blocklist or one of the allowlists. Everything else is optional.

Netoff uses Apple's Network Extension framework — a content filter that macOS itself runs and supervises. It looks at which app is connecting to where. It doesn't read your web pages, messages or files. Requires macOS 15 or later.

Questions

Good to know

Can Netoff see my passwords or messages?

No. Netoff sees which app opens a connection, to which domain or IP address, on which port. It does not read the contents of your traffic. The only data it looks inside is plain DNS answers, in memory, to put a name next to an IP address.

What happens when Netoff isn't running?

The filter lives in a system extension, so your rules keep working even when the Netoff window is closed. Connections that would need your approval are allowed or blocked — your choice in Settings — until Netoff is open again.

Is it a firewall or an antivirus?

No. Netoff controls outgoing connections app by app. It isn't an inbound firewall and it doesn't scan for malware.

Where is my data stored?

Only on your Mac. Rules, the privacy report and the connection log never leave it. See the privacy policy for details, including how to delete everything.

How do I uninstall it?

In Netoff, open Settings and choose “Uninstall Extension…”, then move the app to the Trash. Step by step on the support page.
